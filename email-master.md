## 1.2 Hard compliance rules

These are not stylistic. Breaking one is a regulatory or reputational problem.

- **HOW not WHICH.** Never name a fund, stock, policy, scheme brand, insurer, lender, bank, broker or credit bureau as a recommendation or a comparison. Generic categories are fine. Brands are not.
- **No outcome claims.** No returns, no income figures, no savings projections, no "beats inflation", no "you will be worth X". Not for Milan, not for a student, not implied through a testimonial or a case study.
- **The one permitted arithmetic example.** Doubling over ten years is about 7.18 percent a year. At a thirty percent slab that is roughly 5 percent. Against six percent inflation the real return is roughly minus one percent. This is a worked example on stated assumptions, presented as arithmetic and never as a forecast. Do not extend it into projections of what a reader's money will do.
- **Commissions line is present tense only.** "I don't sell products, so I don't earn commissions." Never "no commissions ever" and never any past-tense absolute. Milan held a mutual fund distributor licence until April 2026 and earned commissions during it. He may say so, and in one email he does.
- **NISM cited by series code only.** X-A and X-B. Never expanded into the words behind the codes. Simplest safe route: do not cite NISM in email at all. No email in this file does.
- **Tax routes to a Chartered Accountant, said out loud in the body**, not only in the footer, in any email that touches tax.
- **Legal routes to a lawyer.** Teach what a rule is and what it does. Never state an individual's position.
- **Testimonials speak to clarity and understanding only.** Never to advice received, returns, savings, or any financial outcome. Written permission required before any testimonial appears.
- **No urgency mechanics.** No countdown, no expiring discount, no struck-through price, no fabricated attendee counts, no fake scarcity. The only permitted scarcity is real: a cohort start date that arrives on its own, one-to-one slots limited by Milan's actual hours, and a price that rises on a published member milestone.
- **The credit bureau credential is structural only.** Permitted: "I know what is on a credit report and how a lender reads it." Banned: any second-person version such as "what a lender sees when they look at you". The second-person framing invites strangers to ask him to look up their file.

### S1-08 — Nurture 1: The number most people never write down
**Trigger:** Day 1 after the session
**Subject:** The number most people never write down
**Preview:** Doubling in ten years is not the win it looks like.

Money that doubles in ten years has grown at about 7.18 percent a year. That is the whole of it. The total looks impressive. The annual rate is what actually describes what happened.

Take tax off at a thirty percent slab and that becomes roughly 5 percent. Set 5 against six percent inflation and the real return is about minus one percent.

The money doubled. What it could buy went down. And the statement looked good the entire time, which is exactly why nobody went back and checked.

This is not pessimism. It is the only honest way to measure anything, because a return before tax and before inflation is not a return, it is a headline.

Take one holding. Annual rate, minus tax, minus inflation. Fifteen minutes. Most people are surprised twice: once by the number, and once by discovering that the holding they were proudest of is not the one doing the work.

### S1-09 — Nurture 2: Insurance is not an investment
**Trigger:** Day 2
**Subject:** Insurance is not an investment
**Preview:** Two jobs, one product, both done badly.

A great deal of money in Indian households sits in products that are trying to do two jobs at once: protect the family if you are not there, and grow money while you are.

The structural problem is not that these products are dishonest. It is that the two jobs want opposite things. Protection wants the largest possible cover for the smallest possible cost. Growth wants the largest possible amount invested with the least possible drag. Combine them and each half constrains the other.

I am not going to tell you what to hold. I am going to give you the question that sorts it out.

**Take each product you own and ask which single job it is doing.** If the answer is both, ask how much cover it actually provides against what your family would need, and separately what it has returned after tax and after inflation. Two numbers. Then judge each half on its own terms.

Most people have never separated the two, which is why the answer has never been obvious.

### S1-10 — Nurture 3: Your emergency cushion, sized properly
**Trigger:** Day 3
**Subject:** Your emergency cushion, sized properly
**Preview:** The one pot that is not meant to perform.

There is one pot of money whose job is not to grow. Its job is to be there, in full, on the worst day, without anything having to be sold at a bad moment.

Almost everybody under-sizes it, and the reason is that idle money feels like a mistake. It is not idle. It is what stops a temporary problem from becoming a permanent one.

**How to size it, rather than what to put it in.** Add up what actually has to be paid every month even if the income stops: the EMI, the rent, the school fee, food, utilities, insurance premiums, anything supporting your parents. That is your monthly floor, and it is usually a good deal lower than your monthly spending, which is the encouraging part.

Then decide how many months of that floor you need. Someone salaried in a stable role needs fewer than someone whose income arrives in lumps. Someone who is the only earner for six people needs more than someone who is not.

The number is yours. The method is the same for everybody.

### S1-11 — Nurture 4: The cost of a five-year delay
**Trigger:** Day 4
**Subject:** The cost of a five-year delay
**Preview:** Time is the one input you cannot buy back.

Every input into a financial outcome can be changed later except one.

You can increase the amount. You can change what it sits in. You can raise your income and redirect the increase. All of that is available at forty, at fifty, at sixty.

Time is not. A year that passes is gone, and it is the input that does the heaviest lifting, because it is the one that compounds the others.

I am not going to put a rupee figure on your delay, because doing that would require me to assume a return I have no business assuming. What I will tell you is the mechanism, and you can run it yourself: an amount that grows for thirty years does considerably more work than the same amount growing for twenty, and the difference is not proportional. It is much larger than the ten year gap suggests.

Which is why "I will sort this out when things are clearer" is the most expensive sentence in personal finance. Things are never clearer. There is only earlier and later.

### S1-12 — Nurture 5: Where most portfolios quietly leak
**Trigger:** Day 5
**Subject:** Where most portfolios quietly leak
**Preview:** Four leaks. None of them feel like losses.

Nobody notices a leak, because a leak does not arrive as a loss. It arrives as a slightly smaller number than there should have been, every year, for a decade.

Four common ones.

**Overlap.** Owning six schemes feels like diversification. If four of them hold substantially the same underlying companies, you own one position wearing six names, with six sets of paperwork.

**Cost.** Every ongoing charge comes out before you see anything. Small annual percentages behave very differently over twenty five years than they look on a one year statement.

**Tax you did not plan for.** The same decision, made with two different holding periods in mind, produces two different post-tax outcomes. If your return calculation ignores this, your number is wrong rather than optimistic. I am not a Chartered Accountant, and your specific position is a conversation to have with one.

**Idle balances.** Money parked in a current or savings account for years because moving it was never anybody's job.

None of these is dramatic. Together they are usually larger than any single decision a person agonises over.

### S1-13 — Nurture 6: One page, not a spreadsheet
**Trigger:** Day 6
**Subject:** One page, not a spreadsheet
**Preview:** Could your family act on what you have built?

Ask yourself a plain question. If you were not there tomorrow, could the people you support find and act on everything you have built?

Not "is it written down somewhere". Could they find it, understand it, and do the next thing.

For most people the honest answer is no, and it has nothing to do with how well organised they are. The information exists across a dozen logins, three institutions, and a memory that belongs to one person.

**The fix is one page, not a system.** What exists, where it is held, who to contact, and where the documents sit. That is it. A single page a spouse or a sibling could pick up and act on.

**Trigger:** Day 8

**Preview:** The name is the smallest part of the decision.

Suppose I gave you something that goes on to triple over five years. Genuinely correct. Three ways you still end up worse off.

**You put in too little.** Two percent of your net worth tripled. Your life is unchanged. You were right and it bought you a story.

**You put in too much.** It fell forty percent in month eight before it tripled, and you were sitting on money you needed within two years, so you sold. You were right and you were not there for it.

**You had no exit written down.** It tripled, then you held another year waiting for more, because nothing told you when the reason you bought it had been satisfied.

Three failures, none of which the tip could have prevented. Size, horizon and exit are decided by your own position, and nobody else can decide them because nobody else knows what you can afford to lose.

That is why the name is the last question, not the first.

### S2-03 — Day 4
**Subject:** Not dishonesty. Structure.
**Preview:** I earned commissions for thirteen years. Let me be useful about it.

There is a lazy version of this argument that says everyone selling you something is out to cheat you. That is not true and it is not the problem.

For thirteen years I held a mutual fund distributor licence and earned commissions. Most of the people I worked alongside were decent, and almost none of them woke up intending to put somebody in the wrong thing.

What actually happens is this. When you are paid for the transaction, the transaction is what you notice. Not dishonesty. Attention. You get very good at answering "which one", and you never develop the habit of asking "should you be doing this at all, how much, and for how long". Nobody pays for that question, so nobody asks it.

I surrendered that licence in April 2026. I do not sell products now, so I do not earn commissions. I mention it not to claim I am purer than anyone, but so you can check my incentive yourself rather than taking my word for it.

Apply that test to everyone, including me.

### S2-04 — Day 7
**Subject:** Count what is already there
**Preview:** Before you add anything.

Almost nobody starts from zero. Before the next purchase, look at what is already in the file.

A policy someone in the family sold you. A scheme a colleague mentioned, started, never reviewed. ### S2-05 — Day 10
**Subject:** It doubled in ten years and you still lost
**Preview:** Run this on one holding.

Money that doubles in ten years has grown at about 7.18 percent a year. The total looks impressive. The annual rate is what describes what happened.

Take tax off at a thirty percent slab: roughly 5 percent. Set that against six percent inflation: the real return is about minus one percent.

The money doubled. What it could buy went down. And the statement looked good the entire time, which is why nobody checked.

This is not a trick or a pessimistic framing. It is the only honest way to measure anything, because a return before tax and before inflation is not a return, it is a headline.

Take one holding you own. Annual rate, minus tax, minus inflation. Fifteen minutes. Most people are surprised twice: once by the number, and once by finding that the thing they were proudest of is not the thing doing the work.

Once you can do this, you can check anything anybody ever recommends, before and after. That is worth considerably more than a name.

### S2-06 — Day 13
**Subject:** Right, and it did not matter
**Preview:** Selection gets the credit. Sizing decides the outcome.

Two people buy the same thing on the same day at the same price. It triples.

The first put in two percent of their net worth. Nothing changed. The second put in forty percent. Their life changed, and they now believe they are good at this.

Same decision, same outcome, entirely different meanings. Had it halved instead, the first would have shrugged and the second would have lost years.

The uncomfortable part is that the second person learned the wrong lesson. They will size the next one the same way, and the next one will not triple.

So the question before any purchase is not "how confident am I". Confidence is not evidence, and it runs highest exactly when you know least about what you do not know.

The question is: **if this goes to zero, what breaks?** If the honest answer is the school fee, or the buffer you keep for your parents' medical costs, the position is too big regardless of how good the idea is.

Write that number down before you buy. Afterwards, you will justify whatever you did. Everybody does.

### S2-07 — Day 16
**Subject:** The gap between what it returned and what you earned
**Preview:** Not the market's fault.

People reliably earn less than the things they own actually returned. This holds across markets and across decades, and it is not caused by fees or by tax.

It is caused by when money arrives and when it leaves. In after a run, when the story is loudest and the price highest. Out after a fall, when the story is worst and the price lowest. The holding performed. The holder did not.

That gap is the largest controllable factor in what anybody ends up with. Larger than product selection. Larger than which stock. By a distance.

Which is why "what should I buy" interests me so little and "what will you do in the month it is down thirty percent" interests me so much. The second decides the outcome of the first.

You fix it with a rule written in advance, by a calmer version of you. Three sentences, dated, kept where you will find them: what you will do if it falls, what would have to be true before you sell, and where the money you need within three years is kept instead. It should not be here at all.

### S2-08 — Day 19
**Subject:** Your biggest position is not a stock
**Preview:** And almost nobody optimises it.

Take what you earn in a year and multiply it by the number of working years you have left. That number is almost certainly larger than everything you own put together.

That is your biggest position, and it is the only one where the return is decided by you rather than by a market.

Nobody treats it that way. Everyone optimises the leftovers and almost nobody questions the number at the top. Income gets treated like weather. It is a variable, and you are accountable for it.

Run the comparison honestly. A meaningful increase in what you earn, compounded across the years you have left, moves your outcome more than almost any single investment decision you are likely to make, and it does not depend on a cycle or on being right about a company.

This gets ignored because it is harder. Researching an investment feels like work and costs nothing socially. Sitting across a table and making the case for what you are worth is exposed, and it can be refused.

That is exactly why it is worth doing.
